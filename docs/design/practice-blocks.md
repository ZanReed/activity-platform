## Problem Statement

An activity is capped at `activity_defaults.duration_min` (24) and a period runs
50–100 minutes, so about half of every period has no plan. D43 fills it with
practice the teacher runs premade: a fluency sprint on facts, and mixed spaced
practice on taught skills. The platform has no notion of a practice session, no
per-student state over time, and no measure of response time.

**What this design does and does not fill.** §17's default 50-minute shape is
"fluency 5 · hook 2–3 · activity · mixed ~15". Slices 1 and 2 cover the five
fluency minutes at most. The larger part, mixed practice, stays unplanned until
banks exist. On day one of term:

| Probe result | Slice 2 built | What the unplanned time holds |
|---|---|---|
| Class median rate at or above the floor | either | The probe (once). Sprint off; its minutes and the mixed minutes are the teacher's until mixed practice ships. |
| Below the floor | yes | The probe, then a daily sprint of about five minutes. Mixed minutes still the teacher's. |
| Below the floor | no | The probe. The sprint is run with an outside fact-fluency tool or on paper until slice 2 ships (see A1). |

So for a class at or above the floor, slices 1–2 do not solve the stated
problem. They are the part that can be built before any bank exists.

## Status Quo

Without this, the unplanned half-period is filled by paper worksheets or a
separate fact-fluency product. Fact fluency is a commodity: XtraMath and Rocket
Math both treat a fact as mastered at a consistent answer inside 3 seconds, and
both adapt per student.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The teacher on day one of a term with a class they do not know. The narrowest
thing worth having is the fact probe: every student answers a short timed set,
and the teacher sees the class median rate against the floor and a
recommendation in the same period.

## Premises (ruled by the author 2026-10-02)

spec review and was RATIFIED with A1 the same day.

1. **The product line moves.** The platform keeps per-student state over time.
   ROADMAP's "not a student-facing app", "not a quiz platform" and spaced
   practice "out of scope until Phase 4+" are amended when slice 1 is built.
2. **Per-student fact state lives in the platform.** REASON RESTATED after the
   cold read. It is not that the probe and the sprint are one machine; they
   share only a timed item runner. It is that D43 puts fluency items inside
   each below-criterion student's mixed practice and seeds the mixed queue from
   diagnostic gaps, so the fact state has to sit beside the skill state. An
   outside tool cannot feed that. An outside tool IS an acceptable interim for
   the sprint alone (A1).
3. **Facts are graded in the browser**; results are saved in checkpoints during
   the session and at its end (AMENDED by the author's G1 ruling in the scope
   review; it first read "upload once at session end").
   Mixed practice and diagnostic skill items stay server-graded. This is a
   recorded exception to server-authoritative grading: a fact holds no secret
   and carries no grade.
4. **A practice session is not an activity.** It cannot be an
   `activity_versions` row.
5. **Raw attempts are the source of truth and are kept for the school year they
   were made in**, then rolled up and pruned. REVISED from "roll up promptly":
   D43 calls the 3-second target a working definition to revisit against
   classroom data, and the raw timings are that data. Mastery can be recomputed
   from attempts WITHIN the current school year; mastery from a pruned year is
   kept as its last value and is not recomputed.
6. **Bank items use the existing markdown format** in a new practice file kind
   keyed by `skill:`, with a per-session seed. ACCEPTED by the curriculum side
   (answer 6) with two format asks, recorded under the deferred pass.
7. **Fact sets are generated from a declared scope**, not authored item by item.
   CONFIRMED by the curriculum side (answer 1): the graph will carry it and
   generate a registry file. It does not exist yet; it is their first D43 piece.
8. **February promises the probe.** REWORDED: the sprint follows behind the
   gate below and may not exist on day one. Mixed practice arrives once two
   chains have ratified banks.

## Recommended Approach — B

### Slice 1: the class-entry fact probe (designed)

**Teacher flow.** On a class, open a probe. Students run it. A results table
shows each student's result and, above it, the class median rate, the floor it
was judged against, and the recommendation. The teacher closes the probe. Next
term's probe is a new row.

**Student flow.** The teacher shares the probe link (a lazy route on the class;
no Home card in this slice). A typing baseline first, then the fact items, then
a done screen showing the student's OWN result and nothing else, in one form:
"You got 24 right out of 30. You skipped 2." Skipped facts count in the 30;
interrupted ones do not. No time, no comparison, no grade.

**The measurement.**
- *Clock.* Starts when the item is painted. Stops at an explicit submit: Enter,
  or the keypad's submit key. There is NO auto-submit at an expected digit
  count, because that would reveal the answer's length.
- *Wrong answers.* The probe gives no feedback and moves on. It measures; it
  does not teach.
- *Typing baseline.* "Type the number shown" trials covering one-, two- and
  three-digit numbers, five per digit count (proposal). Stored on the session
  as medians keyed by (modality, digit count); the individual baseline trials
  are not kept as rows.
- *Modality.* Two values for a clean attempt: `keyboard` (physical keys) and
  `keypad` (the in-app keypad). On a touch device the runner shows the in-app
  keypad and suppresses the OS soft keyboard, so soft-keyboard entry does not
  pass as `keyboard`. An attempt that used both inputs is recorded `mixed`.
  Modality is recorded per attempt. A `mixed` attempt, or one in a modality
  the session has no baseline for, is reported raw only.
- *Raw and net.* Raw response time is always stored. Net time (raw minus the
  baseline for that digit count and modality, floored at zero) is DERIVED at
  read time. **Ruled by the curriculum side (answer 7): the criterion applies to
  NET time, and raw times are kept as well.** An attempt whose RAW time already
  meets the criterion meets it on net too, so it needs no baseline. An attempt
  that fails on raw and has no usable baseline (a `mixed` attempt, or a
  modality with no baseline) cannot be judged; it is stored, shown, and counted
  as `unjudged`, not as a miss.
- *Invalid trials.* An attempt during which the page was hidden or lost focus
  is stored with an `interrupted` flag and left out of every statistic and of
  the done screen's count. An attempt whose raw time exceeds the ceiling is a
  `timeout`: kept, not met, its time capped at the ceiling. The save RPC
  applies the same ceiling: an over-ceiling time is ACCEPTED and marked, never
  a reason to reject the save. The RPC rejects only a malformed payload or one
  over its count caps.
- *Trust model.* Timings and input modality are client-reported and
  unverifiable (the session's `started_at` is the server's time of the first
  save, which the late rule uses). That is accepted because nothing here is a grade: a student
  who tampers changes only their own diagnosis. Whether correctness is also
  taken from the client or re-derived on the server is E2, still open.

**The probe's items** (proposal; the numbers are E8):
- A fixed count, about 30 fact items, sized so baseline plus items fit the
  five-minute budget.
- Sampled across the declared scope's fact families in proportion, seeded by
  the probe id. Every student in a probe gets the SAME items in the SAME
  order, so students' results are comparable.

**The statistic and the recommendation.** Two measures, both ruled on the
curriculum side (answers 2 and 11; quote their decision log, not this):
- *Per fact, for the student's own row and later for mastery.* An attempt is
  `met` when it is correct AND its net time is within the fact family's
  criterion; otherwise `slow` (correct, over time), `wrong`, `skipped`,
  `timeout`, `unjudged` or `interrupted`. A student's row shows the share of items met,
  overall and per family. Every student who submitted anything has a row.
- *Per class, for the recommendation.* A RATE: each student's answers correct
  per minute on the probe, and the class median of those rates, compared with
  an instructional-range FLOOR. Below the floor, the sprint is recommended. The
  floor is a curriculum-owned graph key set with the fact scope; the platform
  holds it as data and never as code.
- The rate's time basis (platform design, told to the curriculum side so the
  floor can be converted against it; they raised that a paper probe's minutes
  include reading and moving to the next item): **the time the student spent
  on the probe, which is the summed raw time of their counted attempts.** The
  per-item clock starts at paint and the next item paints immediately on
  submit, so reading time is inside it and there is no uncounted time between
  items. Raw, not net of the typing baseline, because the floor's source is a
  paper probe whose minutes include writing. Wrong and skipped items' time
  counts. Two cases are kept apart:
  - *Interrupted* (page hidden, focus lost, the tab closed and resumed later):
    the attempt and its time are left out. A resume gap is never counted.
  - *Over the ceiling* (the student sat on one fact): the attempt is `timeout`,
    counts as not met, and contributes the ceiling's worth of time. It is NOT
    dropped, because dropping it would raise the rate of exactly the students
    who are stuck.
  Each attempt also stores its offset from the session start, so time between
  items can be measured later rather than assumed to be zero.
- A student needs a minimum number of counted attempts to have a rate (value:
  E6). Below it their row says so and they are left out of the class median;
  the count of students left out is shown beside the verdict.
- Students removed from the class are left out of the LIVE class statistic and
  of the minimum-students count; their own row is kept. A closed probe's
  snapshot stands as stored.
- Below a minimum number of students the table shows "not enough results yet"
  and no recommendation.
- While a probe is open the statistic is live. **At close it is snapshotted:**
  the class median rate, the floor and criterion VALUES used and the
  recommendation are stored on the probe row. That stored verdict is what "the verdict the teacher
  saw" means; it is never recomputed, so no past criterion revision has to stay
  readable. `scope_rev`, `criterion_rev` and `floor_rev` are fixed when the probe OPENS.

**Accepted additions (scope review, author-ruled 2026-10-02).**
- *Wrong-versus-slow grouping.* Above the per-student rows, the results screen
  groups students per fact family as fluent, slow or inaccurate, derived at
  read time from the per-fact outcomes. No new table. It is what makes a
  below-floor result actionable by hand during A1's stopgap. Proposed rule,
  for the design and eng reviews to confirm: a student is `fluent` in a family
  when their share of items met reaches a threshold held as data; otherwise
  `inaccurate` when wrong and skipped items outnumber slow ones, else `slow`.
  `unjudged` and `interrupted` items are left out. Below a minimum number of
  items in a family the student gets no group there, because three or four
  items are thin evidence; the screen then falls back to ONE overall grouping
  per student, and E8 sizes the item list against families times that minimum.
  The thresholds used are snapshotted with the verdict at close, and attempts
  saved after the close do not enter the grouping.
- *Preview mode.* A lazy public route runs the same runner on a fixed demo
  item list that is labelled a demo, shows the visitor their own count correct
  and rate against labelled demo values, and stores and uploads NOTHING: it
  runs in memory only, with no sessionStorage mirror. It is the rehearsal surface and the account-free target for
  end-to-end tests. Counsel is told of it with E5, since an under-13 student
  could open a public route.

**Probe lifecycle.**
- At most one open probe per class (a partial unique index).
- One counted session per (probe, student). Later saves append to it and a
  repeated save replays; a second, different session is refused.
- A probe auto-closes a set number of days after it opened (proposal: 7) if the
  teacher has not closed it, so a forgotten probe cannot collect mid-term
  results into a class-entry statistic.
- At close, the snapshot is computed over what has been saved by then. A save
  arriving after the close is accepted only for a session that started before
  it AND inside a short grace window (value: E6); otherwise refused. Attempts
  saved after the close appear in the student's row marked late and are
  EXCLUDED from the snapshotted verdict.
- A student who joins while the probe is open may run it. One who joins after
  it closed is not in that probe; that case is D43's late-joiner diagnostic,
  deferred.
- A closed probe is not reopened. The teacher opens a new one.

**Saving, and an unfinished session (author-ruled G1).** The runner saves
progress every few items, when the page is hidden, and at the end, through one
idempotent save keyed on (session, item number). The session row is created by
the first save, so the server knows when it started. A student who does not
finish, or whose probe is closed under them, COUNTS with what they have: the
class rate is correct answers per minute, which is meaningful for a partial
run. The teacher sees who has finished and who is still in progress.

**Skipping (author-ruled G2).** A visible skip control records the fact as
`skipped`: not met, kept apart from `wrong`, with its time counted. A guard
stops a held Enter key, or an Enter pressed before the item could be read,
from skipping or answering the next fact by accident.

**Held results on the device.** Only the attempts not yet saved are held, in
memory and mirrored to `sessionStorage` under a key that includes the student
id and probe id. On reopening the link the runner asks the server for this
student's session and resumes after the last saved item, flagging the one that
was interrupted. The held data is removed once saved and on sign-out. Nothing
is written to `localStorage`, so the next student on a shared device cannot
read it.

**Server.** Postgres only, no Edge Function, so no cold start.
- `class_probes` (id, class_id, opened_by, opened_at, closed_at, scope_rev,
  criterion_rev, floor_rev, fact_grammar_rev, and at close the snapshotted
  class rate, the floor and criterion VALUES used, and the recommendation).
- `practice_sessions` (id, student_id, class_id, probe_id NOT NULL, baseline
  medians keyed by modality and digit count, client key, started_at set by the
  server at the first save, last_saved_at, finished_at). No `kind`
  column in slice 1: nothing would read it. Slice 2 adds what it needs.
- `fact_attempts` (session_id, seq, fact_id, shown, typed, outcome, rt_ms,
  offset_ms from session start, modality).
- One definer RPC saves a batch of attempts: membership checked, idempotent on
  (session, item number), capped on attempts per session and payload size, and
  holding one counted session per (probe, student). An over-ceiling time is
  accepted and flagged, never a reason to reject. A teacher read is gated by the existing
  ownership helpers. RLS calls the helpers; no inlined ownership checks.
- **Deletion.** `student_id` and `class_id` cascade on delete, the
  `section_checks` pattern, so the existing account purge and class deletion
  paths remove this data with no new mechanism. `practice_sessions.probe_id`
  and `fact_attempts.session_id` cascade from their parents.
  `class_probes.opened_by` is SET NULL on a teacher purge (0024's pattern for
  `check_grades.graded_by`), so purging one teacher never deletes a class's
  probe or the sessions under it. A student removed from a class
  (`class_members.removed_at`) keeps their history, as section checks do today.
  The data map records all three tables.

**Fact ids** are deterministic strings (`mul:7x8`, `sq:12`). Ruled on the
curriculum side (answer 9): a turnaround pair is ONE fact with one record, and
the probe and the sprint show it in BOTH orders. So the id is the canonical
pair (smaller operand first) and each attempt also records the order it was
`shown` in. `scope_rev` is minted by the curriculum side's registry and says
WHICH facts are in scope. The platform's id grammar has its own
`fact_grammar_rev`. A probe records both.

**Retention at slice 1.** `retention-policy.md` states the ruled window (the
school year the attempt was made in) and, under its "Mechanics (build state)"
heading, that the prune is NOT built and that ACTUAL retention therefore exceeds
the stated window until the prune is armed. Until then raw attempts leave only
by the existing account and class deletion paths.
