## Problem Statement

An activity is capped at `activity_defaults.duration_min` (24) and a period runs
50–100 minutes, so about half of every period has no plan. D43 fills it with
practice the teacher runs premade: a fluency sprint on facts, and mixed spaced
practice on taught skills. The platform has no notion of a practice session, no
per-student state over time, and no measure of response time.

**What this design does and does not fill.** §17's default 50-minute shape is
"fluency 5 · hook 2–3 · activity · mixed ~15". Slices 1 and 2 cover the five
fluency minutes at most. The larger part, mixed practice, stays unplanned until
banks exist. On day one of term:

| Probe result | Slice 2 built | What the unplanned time holds |
|---|---|---|
| Class median rate at or above the floor | either | The probe (once). Sprint off; its minutes and the mixed minutes are the teacher's until mixed practice ships. |
| Below the floor | yes | The probe, then a daily sprint of about five minutes. Mixed minutes still the teacher's. |
| Below the floor | no | The probe. The sprint is run with an outside fact-fluency tool or on paper until slice 2 ships (see A1). |

So for a class at or above the floor, slices 1–2 do not solve the stated
problem. They are the part that can be built before any bank exists.

## Status Quo

Without this, the unplanned half-period is filled by paper worksheets or a
separate fact-fluency product. Fact fluency is a commodity: XtraMath and Rocket
Math both treat a fact as mastered at a consistent answer inside 3 seconds, and
both adapt per student.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The teacher on day one of a term with a class they do not know. The narrowest
thing worth having is the fact probe: every student answers a short timed set,
and the teacher sees the class median rate against the floor and a
recommendation in the same period.

## Premises (ruled by the author 2026-10-02)

Premises 1, 3, 4, 6 and 7 stand as first ruled. Premises 2 and 5 were revised
after the cold read and RATIFIED by the author the same day (the choice of
approach B, and the retention ruling). Premise 8's rewording came out of the
spec review and was RATIFIED with A1 the same day.

1. **The product line moves.** The platform keeps per-student state over time.
   ROADMAP's "not a student-facing app", "not a quiz platform" and spaced
   practice "out of scope until Phase 4+" are amended when slice 1 is built.
2. **Per-student fact state lives in the platform.** REASON RESTATED after the
   cold read. It is not that the probe and the sprint are one machine; they
   share only a timed item runner. It is that D43 puts fluency items inside
   each below-criterion student's mixed practice and seeds the mixed queue from
   diagnostic gaps, so the fact state has to sit beside the skill state. An
   outside tool cannot feed that. An outside tool IS an acceptable interim for
   the sprint alone (A1).
3. **Facts are graded in the browser**; results are saved in checkpoints during
   the session and at its end (AMENDED by the author's G1 ruling in the scope
   review; it first read "upload once at session end").
   Mixed practice and diagnostic skill items stay server-graded. This is a
   recorded exception to server-authoritative grading: a fact holds no secret
   and carries no grade. NARROWED by the eng review (ER-6): the stored
   `correct` is derived by the server from the probe's item list, and the
   browser's own comparison feeds only the done screen. What stays
   client-reported is timing and input modality.
4. **A practice session is not an activity.** It cannot be an
   `activity_versions` row.
5. **Raw attempts are the source of truth and are kept for the school year they
   were made in**, then rolled up and pruned. REVISED from "roll up promptly":
   D43 calls the 3-second target a working definition to revisit against
   classroom data, and the raw timings are that data. Mastery can be recomputed
   from attempts WITHIN the current school year; mastery from a pruned year is
   kept as its last value and is not recomputed. AMENDED 2026-10-06 (RP-4):
   no summary table ships with the prune, because nothing reads one before the
   sprint; "kept as its last value" is designed in slice 2's pass. What the
   prune keeps is the check's class result.
6. **Bank items use the existing markdown format** in a new practice file kind
   keyed by `skill:`, with a per-session seed. ACCEPTED by the curriculum side
   (answer 6) with two format asks, recorded under the deferred pass.
7. **Fact sets are generated from a declared scope**, not authored item by item.
   CONFIRMED by the curriculum side (answer 1): the graph will carry it and
   generate a registry file. It does not exist yet; it is their first D43 piece.
8. **February promises the probe, for a 13+ class, subject to A3 and A4.**
   REWORDED: the sprint follows behind the gate below and may not exist on day
   one. Mixed practice arrives once two chains have ratified banks. (The
   condition was added by the eng review, ER-25: A3 withdraws the promise for a
   Year 7–8 class and A4 lets the probe slip.)

- **A. Probe only, an outside tool for the sprint permanently.** Ruled out: the

- **B. Probe, then sprint (CHOSEN).** Two slices on one timed runner with a gate

- **C. One practice kernel.** A generic session / item-source / schedule runtime

## Recommended Approach — B

### Slice 1: the class-entry fact probe (designed; eng- and design-reviewed 2026-10-02)

Tags of the form ER-n name the eng-review ruling that set the text, DR-n the
design-review ruling, and CR-n the platform's ruling after the curriculum
side's third to eighth D43 amendments (their items 12 to 30); all three ledgers are near
the end. "Probe" is this document's
word only. It never renders in the product (DR-5): students see "Quick number
facts" and teachers see "Number facts snapshot". "Check" is avoided because
Check is already the activity verb.

**Teacher flow.** The class card gains a "Number facts" disclosure beside
"Activities (n)"; the live view and the results live on their own lazy teacher
route at full width, linked from it (DR-17). To open a snapshot the teacher
picks the year level whose cumulative fact scope it samples ("facts up to Year
N", ER-14; §17 makes the scope cumulative by year and `classes` carries no
year) from a list with one line per year saying what that year adds. The
list is Year 7 to Year 10 only: probes exist for those years, and the Year 7
probe covers every family tagged with an earlier source year (their item 28). Nothing
is picked by default and the choice cannot be changed once open (DR-18).
Students run it. While it is open the teacher sees three counts with fixed
meanings, "In class 28 · Started 23 · Finished 17", refreshed by POLLING the
results RPC every 5 seconds while the tab is visible: the realtime client is
stubbed in this app and throws (CLAUDE.md, standing constraints). A failed
poll keeps the last numbers and says so. Names sit behind "Show who has not
started", and the full-screen view shows only the link and the counts, because
this screen will be on a projector (DR-19). Closing asks first, says how many
students are still working and that it cannot be reopened, and defaults to
"Keep it open" (DR-20). The results then show the verdict, the floor it was
judged against and the recommendation, with student names closed behind "Show
students" on first view (DR-21, DR-22). Next term's snapshot is a new row;
earlier ones are listed with date, year and verdict.

**Student flow.** The teacher shares the link `/facts/:CODE`, where the code
is the class JOIN CODE (DR-1, which replaces ER-15's class-id URL: a UUID
cannot be typed from the board, and the six-character code can). It is a lazy
route, stable for the class; no Home card in this slice. `/facts/demo` is the
preview: join codes exclude the letter O, so "demo" can never be a real code.
Opening the link calls ONE entry RPC, keyed by the code, which returns a named
state. Each state has its own screen, shown BEFORE the runner starts (ER-15,
DR-15, DR-16), built from the join and sign-in cards the app already has:

| State | What the student sees |
|---|---|
| signed out | the route's own gate, the shape `/a/:id` and `/join/:code` use |
| loading | "Getting things ready…", with the shared slow-load line and Retry after a few seconds |
| `not_member` | "Join this class first" and one button, "Join the class and start", which calls the join helper `/join/:code` already uses (so a pending account is promoted and joined in one step) and then re-enters. Also returned for a code that matches no class, where the join then fails with its usual message. The class name is not shown before joining. |
| `none_open` | A WAITING ROOM: "Nothing to do yet. Your teacher has not started this yet. This page checks again by itself." It re-calls the entry RPC every 10 seconds for 10 minutes, then stops and shows "Check now". When a snapshot opens it shows the intro; it never starts by itself. |
| `ready` | the intro, then the typing warm-up |
| `resume` | "Welcome back. You have done 15 of 30. Your answers so far are saved." and "Keep going" |
| `finished` | the done screen |
| `closed` | "Your teacher has finished this. Your 15 answers were saved." |
| a teacher or admin account | "This link is for students", with a way into the demo |
| the entry call failed | "That did not load. Check your connection, then try again." |

Day one therefore needs ONE link: sign in, join, start. Sign-in and joining
are not part of the probe's stated time.

*The intro (DR-6).* The timing is disclosed in words and never shown as a
clock: "About N minutes. It is not marked, and nobody else in the class sees
your answers. Answer each one as quickly as you comfortably can. If you do not
know one, press Skip. Nothing tells you right or wrong as you go. Your teacher
uses the class's results to plan warm-ups, and sees which facts you know
straight away and which take longer." Without the pace line a careful student
reads as slow because of the instructions. The N is computed from the list's
length, at about ten seconds an item rounded up to a minute (35 items is
"about 6 minutes", 60 is "about 10"): their item 24 rules that the probe has NO
time cap and that the student is told the expected time up front.

*The warm-up (DR-13).* Same layout as the runner, with the number where the
expression will be and the hint "Type the number you see, then press Enter.
Keyboard or the keys below: stick with whichever you start with." A mistyped
number stays on screen with "Type 47" until it is typed correctly; feedback is
allowed in the warm-up and nowhere else. Then a card, "Warm-up done. Now the
number facts.", with a Start button.

*What paints an item (DR-10).* An item is painted, and so its clock started,
ONLY as the direct result of a student action: Start, Keep going, Enter or
Skip. Resume shows its card first. When the page comes back from being hidden
the student sees "Paused. You left this page for a moment, so that fact will
not be counted. Nothing is lost." and "Keep going"; the interrupted fact is
NOT asked again.

*The runner (DR-7, DR-8, DR-9, DR-12).* One centred column. From the top: a
thin unlabelled progress bar (the count "12 of 30" exists for screen readers
only, because a visible count lets neighbours compare); the expression, large
(about `clamp(3rem, 12vw, 5rem)`, bold, tabular lining numerals, a true ×);
an answer box of FIXED size, so it never hints at the answer's length; one
line for hints; the keypad, always shown, in calculator order (7-8-9 on top,
matching the in-app calculator) with keys about 60 px tall; and "Skip this
one" as a text button below the keypad, away from Enter. The keypad is ONE
fixed four-column layout (CR-16): the digits in three columns, delete and a
tall Enter in the fourth, and a bottom row of "−", "0" and ".". The minus and
point keys are present only when the probe's scope has a family whose answers
use them; otherwise their cells are blank and nothing else moves. The prompt
is an authored string (their item 21) and may be longer than "7 × 8", for
example "−3 − (−5)" or "0.5 = __ %": the expression shrinks to fit and never
wraps (CR-21). On a short, wide screen the expression and answer
sit left and the keypad right. There is NO animation anywhere in the runner:
the next item replaces the last instantly, which also settles reduced motion.
Nothing shows a clock, and nothing says right or wrong. When an attempt
passes the ceiling the hint line says "Stuck? Skip is fine." The ceiling is on
NET time, so the hint fires when raw time minus the baseline times (the
keystrokes so far plus one for Enter) reaches it, or on raw time when the
session has no baseline (CR-11). By then the attempt can only be a timeout, so
the hint changes no statistic.

*The done screen (DR-3, which changes the copy first approved).* "All done.
You got 24 right. You skipped 2." There is no "out of 30": interrupted facts
are left out, so a denominator would disagree with what the student saw. When
some were left out: "2 were not counted because you left the page." Then
"Your answers are saved. This is not marked." and one button, "Go to your
classes". No time, no comparison, no grade.

**The measurement.**
- *Clock.* Starts when the item is painted. Stops at an explicit submit: Enter,
  or the keypad's submit key. The stop time is the submit event's own
  timestamp, not the time its handler ran. There is NO auto-submit at an
  expected digit count, because that would reveal the answer's length.
- *Wrong answers.* The probe gives no feedback and moves on. It measures; it
  does not teach.
- *Typing baseline (per keystroke; curriculum item 14, CR-1, CR-2, CR-4).*
  "Type the number shown" trials covering one-, two- and three-digit numbers,
  five per digit count. When the probe's scope uses the minus or the point,
  5 of the 15 trials include them (for example "−7" or "0.25"), so the
  baseline covers every key an answer can contain (their item 19, CR-20). The
  baseline is ONE number per modality: the median,
  over the valid warm-up trials in that modality, of the trial's time divided
  by its keystrokes, where the keystrokes are the digits typed PLUS one for
  Enter. It is stored on the session keyed by modality; the individual trials
  are not kept as rows. A mistyped, interrupted or over-ceiling trial is
  REPEATED, at most two extra per digit count (ER-20). A baseline needs six
  valid trials in a modality; with fewer, the session has no baseline there.
  (This replaces the first design's medians per digit count: their ruling
  makes the deduction per keystroke, which removes the answer-length effect
  with one number.)
- *Input and modality (ER-12, refined by DR-2).* There is NO editable input
  element on any device, so no OS keyboard can appear and nothing has to be
  suppressed (`inputmode="none"` is unreliable on iOS Safari, which is why the
  design does not lean on it). The answer box is ONE focusable, non-editable
  region: it is focused at each item, it shows a visible focus ring, and the
  key listener is attached to the runner around it, NOT to the document. A
  document-level digit listener is an always-on single-character shortcut,
  which WCAG 2.1.4 does not allow, and it leaves nothing visibly focused. A
  tap on blank space returns focus to the answer box. Characters come from
  physical keys (number-pad keys included) or from the in-app keypad, which is
  always rendered. An answer is digits with an optional leading minus and an
  optional decimal point, and nothing else (their item 19). "−", from the
  keypad or the physical hyphen key, TOGGLES a leading minus wherever the
  student is in the answer; one decimal point is allowed and a second is
  ignored; Enter needs at least one digit; an answer is at most 8 characters
  (CR-17). The answer box shows a true minus sign. Keypad buttons never take focus from a tap or click; they
  stay reachable by Tab, and a focused button answers only to its own Enter or
  Space. A keypad digit registers on pointer-down and the keypad's Enter on
  click. A held key (auto-repeat) is ignored. Modality is recorded per attempt
  from the SOURCE of its keystrokes: `keyboard`, `keypad`, or `mixed` when one
  attempt used both. A `mixed` attempt, or one in a modality the session has
  no baseline for, is reported raw only. One short check on the school's real
  devices, with a screen reader and switch access included, remains for the
  build.
- *Raw and net (curriculum items 7 and 14; quote their decision log, not
  this).* Raw response time is always stored. Net time is DERIVED at read
  time: raw time minus the baseline per keystroke times the keystrokes in the
  typed answer (every character of the final answer, the minus and the point
  included, plus one when it was submitted with Enter; CR-19), and never less
  than zero. Net time is the basis of BOTH measures below: a fact
  is judged against its family's criterion on net time, and the class rate is
  measured in net time, so the rate, the criteria and the floor share one
  unit. A skip with nothing typed has no deduction. When an attempt's modality
  has no baseline, the session's OTHER baseline is used and the row is flagged
  (CR-3); a `mixed` attempt is treated the same way. A session with no
  baseline at all gets no deduction in the rate, and a correct answer that
  then fails its criterion on raw time cannot be judged: it is stored, shown,
  and counted as `unjudged`, not as a miss. No attempt is ever left out for
  want of a baseline, because leaving it out would raise the rate of a
  student who is stuck.
- *Invalid trials.* An attempt during which the page was hidden or lost focus
  is stored with an `interrupted` flag and left out of every statistic and of
  the done screen's count. An attempt whose NET time exceeds the ceiling is a
  `timeout`: kept, never correct (ER-1, their items 14 and 16), its time
  counted at the ceiling. The ceiling is a curriculum-owned graph key (their
  item 15); the interim value is **15 seconds**, which replaces the 30 seconds
  of ER-2. A timeout is decided when results are READ, like every other
  judgment, and never live: the ceiling is on net time, which depends on the
  keystrokes typed, so no fixed raw cutoff exists, and the runner has no time
  limit in any case (CR-10). The save RPC accepts any time in range and never
  rejects a save for being slow. It rejects a malformed
  payload: an `rt_ms` that is not an integer from 1 to 3,600,000, a `typed`
  value that is not an optional minus, digits and at most one decimal point
  within 8 characters, or an item number outside the probe's list (ER-22,
  CR-17).
- *Trust model.* Timings and input modality are client-reported and
  unverifiable (the session's `started_at` is the server's time of the first
  save). That is accepted because nothing here is a grade: a student who
  tampers changes only their own diagnosis. Correctness is NOT taken from the
  client: the server derives it by comparing `typed` with the answer on the
  probe's stored item list (ER-6, which settles E2). The comparison is NUMERIC
  equality, so ".5", "0.5" and "0.50" all match an answer of 0.5 (CR-18).

**The probe's items.**
- **Mix and length are RULED on the curriculum side (their item 20; quote
  their decision log):** families are weighted equally by default and each
  carries a weight in the registry; each family in scope gets at least 5
  items; the probe's length is the larger of 30 and 5 × the number of
  families in scope. This replaces the first design's "about 30, sampled in
  proportion to the number of facts", which would have filled a Year 9 probe
  with multiplication. The minimum (5) is a graph key.
- **Assembly is RULED too (their item 22, which accepts the platform's
  proposal with one change):** the ruled length is shared out across families
  by weight, rounded so the total is exact (largest remainder); a family with
  fewer facts than its share contributes all of them and the remainder is
  shared among the others by weight; a family's minimum is 5 OR its fact
  count, whichever is smaller.
- **Time is RULED (their item 24):** the probe has no time cap and is not cut
  to fit a slot. It sits OUTSIDE §17's daily period shape; the five-minute
  fluency block is the daily sprint, not the probe. Their item 30 gave the
  lengths, and item 32 added `fact.units` to every year: 8 families at Year
  7 (40 items), 11 at Year 8 (55) and 13 at Years 9 and 10 (65), with item
  30's acceptance still applying. The worst case, every item at the 15 s
  ceiling, is about 16 minutes; they put typical recall time at 4 to 5
  minutes for the 60-item probe. The
  class-entry diagnostic's facts half and skills half may run in separate
  sittings.
- **Materialised on the probe row when it opens (ER-6).** The open RPC samples
  the mirrored fact scope for the chosen year, seeded by the probe id, and
  stores the list: item number, fact id, the order shown, family, the prompt
  as a finished display string and a finished spoken string, and the answer.
  Every client reads that list, so every student gets the SAME items in the
  SAME order whatever app version they load, and the fact scope never has to
  be on a student's device. The browser only renders the strings: the
  importer fills the curriculum side's display and spoken templates once per
  fact when it mirrors a registry revision (their item 21, CR-21), so no
  display grammar runs in the browser or in SQL.
- Each sampled turnaround pair appears once, its operand order set by the
  seed, so both orders occur across the list (E8's platform default). Whether
  a family's pairs are turnarounds is a flag in the registry (their item 18).
- Same items in the same order is ACCEPTED as it stands (ER-21). Neighbours
  see the same sequence, but the probe gives no feedback and is self-paced, so
  screens drift apart within a few items. A resumed session continues; it is
  not a restart and does not replay items.

**The statistic and the recommendation.** Two measures, both ruled on the
curriculum side (answers 2 and 11; quote their decision log, not this).

*What is stored and what is derived (ER-7).* An attempt stores FACTS: what was
typed, whether it was correct, skipped or interrupted, its raw time, its
offset and its modality. JUDGMENTS are derived at read time, by ONE SQL
function that the live view and the snapshot both call: `met`, `slow`,
`wrong`, `skipped`, `timeout`, `unjudged`, `interrupted`. A stored judgment
could not survive a criterion change, and premise 5 needs mastery to be
recomputable from attempts.

- *Per fact, for the student's own row and later for mastery.* An attempt is
  `met` when it is correct AND its net time is within the fact family's
  criterion; otherwise `slow` (correct, over time), `wrong`, `skipped`,
  `timeout`, `unjudged` or `interrupted`. A student's row shows the share of
  items met, overall and per family. Every student who submitted anything has
  a row.
- *Per family, for each student (their item 23; quote their decision log).* A
  family is MET when at least 80% of its counted items meet the family's
  criterion (the same graph key as item 13's facts-met threshold); otherwise
  NOT MET. It is NOT JUDGED when interruptions leave it with fewer counted
  items than its minimum (item 22). The teacher sees met, not met or not
  judged per family for each student NOW, in the row's detail, so strategy
  work can start in class before the sprint exists. This is a per-family
  reading, not a per-family grouping: the student still has one group
  (CR-7).
- *Per class, for the recommendation (curriculum items 11, 12 and 14).* A
  RATE: each student's answers correct per minute on the probe, and the class
  median of those rates, compared with the FLOOR for the probe's year level.
  Below the floor, the sprint is recommended.
- *The floor is CALCULATED, not authored (item 12, CR-5).* floor(N) = k × 60 ×
  Σ n_f ÷ Σ (n_f × t_f), where n_f is the number of items from family f in
  the probe and t_f is that family's criterion in seconds: the rate of a
  student answering every fact exactly at its criterion, times one factor k.
  The curriculum side authors only k. The platform computes the floor WHEN THE
  PROBE OPENS, from that probe's actual item list and the criteria copied at
  open, and stores it on the probe row; the mirror holds k under the registry's
  revision.
- *The rate's time basis (item 14, which SUPERSEDES the raw-time basis of B-15
  and of the eng review):* correct answers ÷ the summed NET time of the
  counted attempts, in minutes. The per-item clock starts at paint and the
  next item paints immediately on submit, so reading time is inside it. Wrong
  and skipped attempts count in the time and add nothing to the numerator.
  Two cases are kept apart:
  - *Interrupted* (page hidden, focus lost, the tab closed and resumed later):
    the attempt is left out of both the count and the time. A resume gap is
    never counted.
  - *Over the ceiling* (the student sat on one fact): the attempt is `timeout`
    and counts at the ceiling's time, net of the baseline; with nothing typed,
    the full ceiling. It is NOT dropped, because dropping it would raise the
    rate of exactly the students who are stuck.
  Each attempt also stores its offset from the session start, so time between
  items can be measured later rather than assumed to be zero.
- *"Correct" has ONE meaning everywhere (ER-1, item 14, CR-6):* the answer is
  right AND was given within the ceiling. A `timeout` is never correct,
  whatever was typed, in the rate's numerator, in the done screen's count and
  in the grouping's accuracy. A skip is not correct.
- *Who has a rate (ER-3, CR-8).* A student with at least 10 counted attempts
  OR at least 60 seconds of counted (net) time. An attempts-only minimum would
  drop a slow student who worked the whole period and finished eight items,
  whose low rate is well measured. Below both, the row says so and the student
  is left out of the class median and of the grouping; the count of students
  left out is shown beside the verdict. A student whose attempts were all
  skipped has a rate of zero and a row.
- Students removed from the class are left out of the LIVE class statistic and
  of the minimum-students count; their own row is kept. A closed probe's
  snapshot stands as stored.
- Below 5 students with a rate (ER-23) the table shows "not enough results
  yet" and no recommendation. A probe that closes in that state stores that
  result.
- The net basis removes most of the keyboard-versus-keypad gap the raw basis
  carried, since each modality has its own baseline. Modality is still stored
  per attempt so what remains can be measured from real data.
- **Parameters are fixed when the probe OPENS.** The per-family criterion
  values, k and the floor computed from them, the ceiling, the two grouping
  thresholds (all curriculum-owned graph keys the platform mirrors and never
  invents; their item 15) and the platform's own minimums are COPIED onto the
  probe row at open, beside `registry_rev` and `fact_grammar_rev`. Every read
  uses the row's values and nothing else.
- **The verdict is snapshotted at close:** the class median rate, the counts,
  and the recommendation are stored on the probe row and never recomputed.
  That stored verdict is what "the verdict the teacher saw" means. Because
  nothing is saved after the close and the parameters were fixed at open, the
  snapshot's remaining job is to hold the verdict steady against later roster
  changes, account purges and the prune.

**Accepted additions (scope review, author-ruled 2026-10-02).**
- *Wrong-versus-slow grouping.* Above the per-student rows, the results screen
  groups students as fluent, slow or needs strategy (their item 16 renamed
  "inaccurate"), derived at read time from the per-fact outcomes. No new table. It is what makes a below-floor result
  actionable by hand during A1's stopgap.
  - **The rule is RULED on the curriculum side (item 13, which answers ER-5;
    quote their decision log).** Accuracy first, in this order: `needs strategy`
    when accuracy on the probe is below 90%; otherwise `fluent` when at least
    80% of the probed facts meet their family's criterion; otherwise `slow`.
    Their item 16 accepts CR-6 as proposed: a right answer past the ceiling
    counts against accuracy, as wrong answers and skips do.
  - The platform's definitions under that rule (CR-6, CR-7, CR-8; sent to them
    in B-17): accuracy is correct ÷ counted attempts, with "correct" as defined
    above, so a skip and a timeout both count against it. The probed facts are
    the counted attempts. `unjudged` items count as not met. `interrupted`
    items are left out. Only a student who has a rate is grouped.
  - ONE grouping per student. There is NO per-family grouping (CR-7, which
    replaces ER-4's "per family where the count allows"): at 90%, a family
    with five items puts a student in "needs strategy" on one slip. The per-family
    numbers stay in the row's detail.
  - The 90% and 80% values are graph keys the platform mirrors (their item
    15, which answers B-17), copied onto the probe row at open.
  - A student whose typing speed could not be allowed for (CR-3) carries that
    flag NEXT TO THEIR GROUP, not only on their row: measured on raw time
    against a net-time floor they lean towards "slow", and a teacher should

- *Preview mode.* A lazy public route, `/facts/demo`, runs the same runner on

**Probe lifecycle.**
- At most one open probe per class (a partial unique index).
- One session per (probe, student), enforced by a unique index. There is no
  client key (ER-11): a reload resumes the same session, and two tabs write to
  one session, where the first write for an item number wins and a repeated
  save is a no-op.
- **Auto-close is by rule, with no scheduled job (ER-9).** `closes_at` is set
  at open (7 days later, ER-23). Every RPC treats a probe past `closes_at` as
  closed. The snapshot is written by whichever comes first: the teacher's
  close, the teacher's next results read, or the next open on that class. All
  three call one idempotent finalise function. A forgotten probe therefore
  cannot collect mid-term results, and there is no executor to fail.
- **A save after the close is refused (ER-10).** The late-save path and its
  grace window are CUT: they existed for "upload once at the end", which G1
  replaced. With a save after every item, a student whose probe closes under
  them loses at most the item in flight, and sees the `closed` screen.
- Close and save cannot interleave wrongly: a save takes a SHARE lock on the
  probe row and reads its state under that lock; a close takes an UPDATE lock.
  So a close waits for saves in flight and then snapshots them, and a save
  that starts after a close sees it closed. "The snapshot contains exactly the
  attempts saved before the close" holds by construction, and the verify
  script proves both orders.
- A student who joins while the probe is open may run it. One who joins after
  it closed is not in that probe; that case is D43's late-joiner diagnostic,
  deferred.
- A closed probe is not reopened. The teacher opens a new one.
- Opening and closing a probe write audit rows, as `class.create` does
  (ER-24).

**Saving, and an unfinished session (author-ruled G1).** The runner saves
after EVERY item, coalescing while a request is in flight, and again when the
page is hidden (ER-19), through one idempotent save keyed on (session, item
number). The session row is created by the first save, so the server knows
when it started. A student who does not finish, or whose probe is closed under
them, COUNTS with what they have: the class rate is correct answers per
minute, which is meaningful for a partial run. The teacher sees who has
finished and who is still in progress. The save queue refreshes the session
before firing after a long gap, as the viewer's check queue does.

Saving is SILENT in the runner (DR-14): no per-item indicator, and no prompt
ever interrupts an item. If saves have been failing for more than 10 seconds
one quiet line appears under the keypad: "Not connected. Keep going; your
answers are kept on this device." Retries with a visible state, and any
request to sign in again, happen only on the done screen: "Saving your last
answers…", then "We could not save your last 2 answers. Keep this page open
and we will keep trying." with "Try now". A save refused because the snapshot
closed goes straight to the `closed` screen.

**Skipping (author-ruled G2).** A visible skip control records the fact as
`skipped`: not met, kept apart from `wrong`, with its time counted. Skipping
happens ONLY through that control: Enter on an empty answer does nothing and
shows "Type an answer, or press Skip." (DR-11; the scope review's "empty
answer -> skipped" line is superseded). A guard ignores Enter, the keypad's
Enter AND Skip in the first 300 ms after an item is painted (a client
constant, ER-23), so a held key or a double tap cannot answer or skip the next
fact by accident.

**Held results on the device.** Only the attempts not yet saved are held, in
memory and mirrored to `sessionStorage` under the existing
`activity-viewer:` prefix, in a key that includes the student id and probe id.
`signOutEverything` already purges that prefix from both storages, so the
sign-out clearing needs NO new eager code (ER-19, which closes R3-18's second
half). On reopening the link the entry RPC returns this student's session and
the runner resumes after the last saved item. The held data is removed once
saved. Nothing is written to `localStorage`.

**Server.** Postgres only, no Edge Function, so no cold start.
- *The fact-scope mirror (ER-13).* The importer's registry pass expands the
  curriculum registry's families and ranges into facts ONCE, which is the only
  place the fact grammar runs, and writes them keyed by revision: fact id,
  year, family, operands, answer, and the filled display and spoken strings
  the criterion per family, the floor factor k (CR-5) and the other graph keys
  (the two grouping thresholds, the ceiling, the minimum items per family and
  the practice window, which only the sprint reads; their item 25), ALL under
  ONE `registry_rev`: the registry's header carries one revision id that
  covers scope, criteria and the single values together, and any change to
  any of them mints a new one (their items 18 and 25). The first design's
  three revision columns are gone. When it fills a template (`{a}`, `{b}` and
  `__` for the blank; their item 26) the importer applies the display rules
  itself, a true minus sign and brackets round a negative second operand, and
  says the numbers in the spoken form: the spoken template supplies only the
  words for the operation and the sign, with "negative" for a number's sign
  and "minus" for subtraction, so −3 − (−5) is "negative three minus negative
  five". The two fraction families add a third placeholder,
  `{b-fraction-name}`, which speaks the denominator as a fraction name
  ("three quarters"); its words come from a table in the registry, singular
  when `{a}` is 1 and plural otherwise, and an unlisted denominator fails the
  import (CR-22). The importer also stops if its expansion of a family gives a
  different fact count from the one the registry carries (CR-23). Rows are
  insert-only
  and a revision's rows never change, so a rename or retirement cannot leave
  an orphan the way the upsert-only misconception mirror can. What the
  registry carries is ruled (their item 18: year scope as "what Year N adds"
  plus generated cumulative lists; per family a source year (their item 28;
  Phase 2 families are tagged Year 5 or 6), an id, name, operation, exact
  ranges, criterion, turnaround flag, weight, templates and strategy text; an
  optional line per year for the picker, composed from family names when it
  is missing). The exact columns are settled when the file exists.
- `class_probes` (id, class_id, opened_by, opened_at, closes_at, closed_at,
  year_level, registry_rev, fact_grammar_rev, the
  parameter values copied at open, the item list, and the snapshot written at
  close).
- `practice_sessions` (id, probe_id NOT NULL, student_id, class_id, the
  per-keystroke baseline keyed by modality (CR-1), app build (ER-24), started_at
  set by the server at the first save, last_saved_at, finished_at). Unique on
  (probe_id, student_id). No `kind` column and no client key in slice 1:
  nothing would read them.
- `fact_attempts` (session_id, seq, student_id, fact_id, shown, typed,
  correct, skipped, interrupted, rt_ms, offset_ms from session start,
  modality, saved_at). `fact_id`, `shown` and `correct` are filled by the
  server from the probe's item list; the client sends only the item number,
  the typed answer, the time, the offset and the modality, so a fact outside
  the list cannot be submitted. `student_id` is carried on the row (ER-17) so
  the data-map guard, which sweeps for person columns, sees the table that
  holds response times, and so the purge can count what it deletes.
- *RPCs*, all security definer with a pinned search path and a grant stanza
  each (0009's standing rule): open a probe, close a probe, the student entry
  read, the save, and the teacher results read. The statistic and the finalise
  step are internal functions those call. The student is always taken from the
  session, never from a parameter.
- *Access (ER-8).* RLS is enabled and forced on all three tables with NO
  select policy for students, and no write policy for anyone: the absence of a
  policy is the control, as on `section_checks` writes. A student reads an
  open probe only through the entry RPC, which returns the item list and their
  own session state and never the class verdict. The teacher reads through the
  results RPC, gated by `is_class_teacher`.
- *Caps are structural.* An item number must exist in the probe's list and
  (session, item number) is the primary key, so a session can never hold more
  rows than the probe has items.

**Deletion and retention at slice 1 (ER-16, ER-18).** The first draft said the
existing account and class deletion paths remove this data "with no new
mechanism". Both halves were wrong, read against the code:
- `purge_soft_deleted` deletes a purged student's section checks EXPLICITLY,
  "so destroying student work is a counted act" (0022, 0034); it does not ride
  the cascade. So slice 1's migration RE-CREATES `purge_soft_deleted` with an
  explicit, counted delete of practice data for accounts past their window.
  Practice data does NOT block an account purge the way retained section
  checks do. This touches the nightly job that deletes student work, so its
  verify script and a liveness proof ride the same migration.
- Nothing purges class rows: a class is only ever soft-deleted, and
  `retention-policy.md` already says the mechanism is not built. So a
  soft-deleted class KEEPS its probes, sessions and attempts until the prune
  slice, and the retention policy says so in plain words.
- Foreign keys: `student_id` cascades from `users`; `practice_sessions.probe_id`
  and `fact_attempts.session_id` cascade from their parents; `class_id`
  cascades from `classes` (inert until a class purge exists).
  `class_probes.opened_by` is SET NULL on a teacher purge, so purging one
  teacher never deletes a class's probe or the sessions under it. A student
  removed from a class (`class_members.removed_at`) keeps their history, as
  section checks do today.
- Windows: raw attempts are kept for the school year they were made in
  (premise 5). Session rows, which hold the typing baselines, are pruned WITH
  their attempts. The `class_probes` row and its snapshot hold no student
  identity and are kept for the life of the class (ER-18).
- `retention-policy.md` states those windows and, under its "Mechanics (build
  state)" heading, that the prune is NOT built and that ACTUAL retention
  therefore exceeds the stated window until it is armed. The data map records
  all three tables and the mirror.

**Fact ids** are deterministic strings (`mul:7x8`, `sq:12`). Ruled on the
curriculum side (answer 9): a turnaround pair is ONE fact with one record, and
the probe and the sprint show it in BOTH orders. So the id is the canonical
pair (smaller operand first) and each attempt also records the order it was
`shown` in. `registry_rev` is minted by the curriculum side's registry and says
WHICH facts are in scope, with which criteria and values. The platform's id grammar has its own
`fact_grammar_rev`, which names the importer's expander. A probe records both.

### Responsive and accessibility

- Keyboard alone: digits, Backspace, Enter on the answer box; Tab reaches Skip,
  then the keypad. The answer box always shows the app's focus ring.
- Screen readers: each item announces its spoken form ("seven times eight",
  "twelve squared") once; typed digits are announced politely; the live
  counts announce only when "Finished" changes, never on every poll.
- Touch targets: keypad keys about 60 px, every other control at least 44 px.
- Layout: one column up to about 22rem wide; on a screen under about 620 px
  tall and over 640 px wide, expression and answer left, keypad right. The
  runner never scrolls on a 1366 by 768 Chromebook. No orientation lock.
- Contrast and colour: the existing tokens, which are AA in light and dark.
  The verdict uses an icon and words; colour is never the only signal.
- Motion: none in the runner.
- Known limit (DR-23): the clock starts at paint, before speech finishes, so a
  screen-reader or switch user is measured slow by construction. The results
  screen says so.
- Sign-in round trip: the redirect allowlist is a wildcard on the origin
  (HISTORY, Supabase Auth setup), so `/facts/*` needs no dashboard change.

### Roll-up and prune: as ruled and as built (2026-10-06)

The design pass was re-derived against 0045 to 0047 and the live database
(two closed checks, two sessions, 163 attempts, one class, none deleted; no
job other than the purge and the analytics run). Two things the earlier plan
did not say came out of that reading:

- `fact_probe_stat` lists EVERY class member, so a check whose sessions were
  removed would have shown each student as "Did not start". The results read
  now returns no student rows for a pruned check and the screen says why.
- Removal has to be by CHECK. A check's sessions are read together (the
  per-family table, the live class numbers), so removing some of them would
  leave a check that reads as a smaller class. The date therefore lives on
  the check.

**The author's rulings (eight numbered questions, each answered with the
recommended option).**

| # | Ruling |
|---|---|
| RP-1 | **E1: "the school year" is an explicit end date per class** (`classes.school_year_ends_on`), given by the teacher. Each check copies it at open as `keep_until`, like its other parameters, so moving the class's date later never extends a past year's data. Nothing is inferred from a calendar or a timezone. |
| RP-2 | The date is asked in the open-snapshot screen the first time a class opens a check, prefilled with a guess the teacher confirms (18 December for New Zealand and Australian timezones, 30 June otherwise), and changeable there afterwards. No check opens without one, or after it has passed. |
| RP-3 | 30 days of grace: `keep_until` = the end date + 30 days. |
| RP-4 | **No roll-up table ships now.** The per-student per-fact summary has no reader until the sprint; it is designed in slice 2's pass, with mastery. Premise 5 is amended. A de-identified calibration summary is filed with a trigger (TODOS). |
| RP-5 | A soft-deleted class: its checks' sessions and attempts are removable 30 days after the deletion, or at `keep_until` if that comes first. The check rows and class results stay with the class row (no class purge exists; building one is not in this slice). |
| RP-6 | The teacher sees the date ahead ("Students' answers and timings are kept until …") and, after removal, a notice with the date, the class result, and no student panels. `class_probes.pruned_at` records it. |
| RP-7 | 0035's discipline and a checklist: dry-run by default, service-role only, unscheduled, check by check. No extra mechanical switch. Arming needs counsel Q11(b), a liveness proof at production values, dry-run reports read on live, and the author's explicit yes. |
| RP-8 | Backstop: `keep_until` is never more than 400 days after the check opened (the dormant-student figure). Checks opened before 0048 were backfilled at exactly that. An end date more than 400 days ahead is refused. |

**As built (migration 0048).** `set_class_year_end` (the teacher's one
write; today to 400 days ahead). `open_fact_probe` raises
`school_year_end_missing` or `school_year_ended` and stamps `keep_until`.
`fact_probe_overview` returns the class's date and each check's `keep_until`
and `pruned_at`; `fact_probe_results` returns the two dates and, for a pruned
check, an empty student list. `prune_fact_practice(p_dry_run default true)`:
a check is a candidate when it is not already pruned, is closed or due to
close, and either `keep_until` has passed or its class was deleted more than
30 days ago. An armed run locks each candidate, finalises a due one first
(so the class result is written from the data before the data goes), deletes
its attempts and sessions, stamps `pruned_at`, and writes one
`fact_practice.prune` audit row with the counts and the reason. The student
entry needed no change: a closed check with no session already reads as
"none open".

`verify-0048.sql` (registered): the catalog posture, including a row that
fails if any cron job names the prune; then, at production values, the end
date's refusals, `keep_until` at open with the grace and the backstop, and
the prune's matrix (a dry run deletes nothing; an open check is never a
candidate; 29 days after a class deletion is not enough and 31 is; the armed
run removes exactly the candidates' rows and keeps a control check's; the
audit counts; a second run finds nothing; the teacher's and the student's
reads afterwards). The prune's report is global, so the block measures the
difference its own fixtures make against a baseline.

Compliance in the same commit: data-map `draft-15`, retention-policy
`draft-11`, and counsel Q11(b) reworded to say the mechanism exists and is
switched off.

Mutations, each red then restored: the prune's "closed or due" clause
removed (B5: an open check became a candidate); `pruned_at` not read by the
results screen (the removed-notice test); the class's stored end date not
read by the open screen (the "opens without asking again" test);
`keep_until` not rendered (the kept-until test).

**Not built, by ruling:** the per-student summary (RP-4, slice 2); a class
purge (RP-5); any schedule (RP-7).
